Atlas

Single-image world reconstruction: one photograph in, one inhabitable diorama out

J. Garcia1   1Institute of General Acceleration, Chicago

Abstract

Atlas lifts a complete three-dimensional world out of a single 2D photograph. The system reads the image's palette, luminance field, and texture statistics, then grows terrain, water, and structures in the photograph's own colors. Every reconstruction is unique to its source image and fully deterministic: the same photo always grows the same world. Runs entirely on device.

Method

  1. Palette extraction. The photograph is quantized to a small set of representative colors that become the world's entire material library.
  2. Elevation from luminance. Bright regions rise, dark regions sink; the image's light field becomes terrain relief on a circular diorama base.
  3. Procedural growth. Structures sprout where the image is most saturated or detailed, colored by their nearest palette entry, and the world animates into place.

Limitations

Atlas reconstructs the world the photograph implies, not the world it captured. A photo of your lunch becomes a landscape in lunch colors. The model considers this a feature.
